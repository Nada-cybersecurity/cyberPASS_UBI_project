# 2-minute video: storyboard, clicks and narration

## Setup (5 minutes before recording)
- Open the live demo in Chrome, window 1440 x 900, zoom 100%, light theme, bookmarks bar hidden.
- Click **Reset demo** (top right) and confirm. This restores AtlasTech before any evidence is uploaded.
- Go back to the landing page (click the logo).
- Recorder: OBS Studio or the built-in Windows (Win+Alt+R) / macOS (Cmd+Shift+5) recorder, 1080p, microphone only, no music.
- Read the narration once aloud with a timer. Target pace about 135 words per minute.

## Demo data (exact)
AtlasTech SARL (fictional), Rabat, Morocco. Software/SaaS, 35 employees, 3 administrators, 5 contractors. Customers in Morocco, France and Germany. AWS (EU region) and Microsoft 365. Customer personal data. Staff in Rabat can access EU-hosted data. Evidence file: `Access_Control_Policy.pdf` (built into the app).

## Narration (260 words)

> A Moroccan software company just landed its first big European customer. Then the security questionnaire arrives: can you prove your cybersecurity and data protection? Which laws apply? Where does your data go? What evidence counts?
>
> This is AfriEU CyberPass. Meet AtlasTech: a SaaS company in Rabat, thirty-five employees, customers in France and Germany, hosted on AWS, handling customer personal data. Every answer shapes the result. If engineers in Rabat can't reach EU data, one requirement disappears. Switch it back on, and it returns.
>
> The platform shows its reasoning. Moroccan law 09-08 applies to processing in Morocco. GDPR reaches AtlasTech through its customers' contracts, not automatically. And because staff in Rabat can access EU-hosted data, it flags a potential international transfer: Morocco has no EU adequacy decision. Every result has a condition, a source and a review flag.
>
> Now evidence. AtlasTech uploads its access control policy. Verdict: partially supported. The rules are written, but there's no multi-factor authentication and no proof that access reviews happen. A policy is intent, not implementation.
>
> So the risk stays high: unauthorised access to the customer database. Instead of fifty gaps, AtlasTech gets three priorities: enforce MFA, run quarterly access reviews, test incident response. Each has an owner, a cost and a deadline.
>
> Finally, the EU Customer Security Passport: maturity score, ISO 27001 readiness, requirements, data flows and open gaps, with a tamper-evident fingerprint. A European buyer sees the same data as a supplier risk rating and a ready evidence request.
>
> AfriEU CyberPass turns cybersecurity compliance from a barrier to cross-border business into a business passport.
>
